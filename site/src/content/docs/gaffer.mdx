Reception is the desk you talk to. The gaffer is the parent agent for one
**job**, and it keeps working when you walk away.

You ask reception for something. It files a job and you close the lid. The
gaffer starts each part as a [session](/docs/sessions), starts the next part
when the one it depends on has landed, restarts anything that died, and asks
you on the pull request when it needs you. When you open the laptop again,
reception asks where things are. It never kept track itself.

```bash
factory job add --line lyr "searchAfter in layer and layer-pro"
factory jobs                    # every job: parts, pull requests, what it's waiting on
factory job show ID
```

## The job is the state

A job is a directory where the jobs live: the ask, its parts and what each
part runs after, a done-when check, and a ceiling on wakes, days and dollars.
The gaffer's context is only a cache of it. If a gaffer is lost, a new one
reads the job and carries on.

## Asleep costs nothing

A tick runs every minute and calls no model. It collects what happened, such
as a session that finished, died or asked a question, a pull request that
merged, or a reply from you. It wakes the gaffer only for jobs where something
changed. A factory with nothing to do makes no model calls.

## What it won't do

It never merges and never approves anything on your behalf. It files and runs
the work, and you review what comes out.

## One machine, or two

In the open build, everything lives on one Mac: reception, the jobs, the
gaffers and the sessions, acting as whoever is logged in. In
[pro](/#get), reception runs on your laptop as a client. The jobs, gaffers and
sessions live on the always-on host as [hevbot](/docs/hevbot). Pro also lets
you file a job by moving a Linear issue to Todo from your phone, and sends
alerts to Slack. The gaffer is the same in both. What changes is only where
it runs.
