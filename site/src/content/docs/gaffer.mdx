A gaffer is the parent session for one **job**. [Reception](/docs/reception)
files the job and returns. The gaffer starts each part as a
[session](/docs/sessions), starts the next part when the one it depends on has
landed, restarts anything that died, and comments on the pull request when it
needs an answer. Reception never tracks the job. It reads the job's state when
you ask.

```bash
factory job add --line lyr "searchAfter in layer and layer-pro"
factory jobs                    # every job: parts, pull requests, what it's waiting on
factory job show ID
```

## The job is the state

A job is a directory on the machine the jobs live on. It holds the ask, its
parts and what each part runs after, a done-when check, and a ceiling on how
many times it wakes and for how many days. The gaffer's context is only a
cache of it. If a gaffer is lost, a new one reads the job and carries on.

## Asleep costs nothing

A tick runs every minute and calls no model. It collects what happened: a
session that finished its turn, failed or died, a pull request that merged, or
a reply on one. It wakes the gaffer only for jobs where something changed. A
factory with nothing to do makes no model calls.

## Merging and releases

A part's pull request merges into the next release, the default branch, once
its CI workflow is green (checked by name) and its acceptance checks pass. Its
session merges it, or else the gaffer does, without waiting for your review.
The gaffer never approves anything, and never publishes, undrafts or tags a
release. You review what landed, and you own releases.

## Where it runs

<Edition only="oss">

On the one Mac, next to reception and the sessions, acting as whoever is
logged in. The gaffer is the same in pro. Only where it runs changes.

</Edition>

<Edition only="pro">

On the always-on host, next to the jobs and the sessions, as the
[bot account](/docs/identity). Reception on the laptop reaches it through
`factory-remote`.
Pro adds two ways for jobs to reach you:

- **Intake.** Moving a Linear issue to Todo files a job, through
  `factory-intake`. The gaffer comments on the issue as it goes.
- **Alerts.** A job that's waiting on you, stuck, done, or past its ceiling
  posts to Slack, through `factory-notify`.

</Edition>
